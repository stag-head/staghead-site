Supply you should not touch

Standard planning will happily suggest moving a purchase order that is already on a ship. ForecastCentral knows what cannot change, plans around it, and tells you why.

§ 01 · What it does

Supply locks in practice

  • 01

    Supply status

    A short list you control, installed with Open, Confirmed, In-Transit and Arrived. Each status says whether planning may change it.

  • 02

    Lock rules

    Received or partly received, shipped by the vendor, inside the demand time fence, production started, or locked by a planner. Vendor confirmed and prepayment invoiced can be switched on if you want them.

  • 03

    On the water, without extra locations

    An order shows as on the water when a prepayment has been invoiced, a shipped date or vendor shipment number is filled in, or a shipment reference exists. No in-transit location is needed to keep it visible.

  • 04

    Shipment reference

    Taken from the vessel or flight, container number or bill of lading on the order. Add-ons, such as a landed cost module, can supply their own through a published event.

  • 05

    Inbound pipeline

    Stock that sits in an in-transit location is treated as a locked receipt at its destination on the expected arrival date, never as available stock and never counted twice.

  • 06

    Locked and explained

    A locked order is never suggested for change, and the explanation says which rule locked it.

§ 02 · In a real situation

The container on the water

Example

A purchase order for 12 pallets left the supplier last week. The vendor has sent a shipment number and the order is marked In-Transit.

A standard run sees a due date that has not arrived and suggests expediting it or buying more. ForecastCentral sees the status and the shipment number, treats the order as locked supply arriving on its expected date, and plans around it. If the planner opens the item, the explanation names the rule that locked the order.
