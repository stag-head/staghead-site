§ 01 · The problem

Where standard planning gets in the way

The planning engine in Business Central does a reasonable job for simple supply chains. For manufacturers and distributors with real volumes, the same few things come up again and again.

01

Capacity is assumed

Every order is scheduled as if machines never run out of time. Planners smooth the overloads by hand.

02

Locations work alone

It suggests buying at one location without looking at spare stock sitting at another.

03

Vendor terms are ignored

Minimum order values and price breaks are not part of the sums, so buyers end up with many small orders.

04

Small changes cause noise

A delivery date that moves a day or two can set off a flood of reschedule messages.

Underneath all four is a bigger one. When the system says "buy 400", nobody can see why, so nobody trusts it.

§ 02 · What ForecastCentral does

Fewer surprises, and reasons for the rest

  • 01

    Explains every suggestion

    Quantity, date and message each show why they exist, step by step, down to the source records.

  • 02

    Uses spare stock first

    Transfers between locations are considered before anything new is bought.

  • 03

    Leaves locked supply alone

    Received, shipped and in-production orders are never suggested for change, and the reason is shown.

  • 04

    Builds sensible vendor orders

    Minimum values, multiples, price breaks and container fill are part of the maths.

  • 05

    Keeps plans calm

    Frozen, Firm and Open zones and tolerances stop small shifts from creating work.

  • 06

    Plans from budgets and history

    Versioned budget scenarios sit next to a clean demand history, and nothing is changed without a record.

§ 03 · Standard methods

Textbook planning, with the sums shown

Nothing here is a secret formula. ForecastCentral uses the methods planners already know, shows the working, and uses the same terms in the explanation on every suggestion.

ABC and XYZ

Sort items by value and by predictability

A is the items that make up the first 80 percent of demand value. X is demand steady enough to have a coefficient of variation up to 0.5. Each class gets its own policy.

 Safety stock

Cover late deliveries and surprise demand

z × √(L × σd² + d² × σL²)
Or a simple number of weeks of forward cover. Your choice, per policy.

 Reorder point and EOQ

When to order, and how much

EOQ = √(2 × D × S ÷ (C × h))
With minimum quantities, multiples and maximums applied in a fixed, explained order.

 Forecast consumption

Count each unit of demand once

Real orders use up the plan around them, so a big order does not sit on top of a forecast for the same sales.

 Time fences

Frozen, Firm and Open

Changes are held back close to today and allowed further out. Defaults are 14 and 42 days, and you can change them.

 Accuracy and bias

Know how good the plan really is

WAPE = Σ|actual − plan| ÷ Σ actual
Measured at one to five months ahead, by item, class and planner.

§ 04 · A planning week

The system works overnight, people handle the exceptions

STEP 1 · ONCE

Set up

Load two or three years of history, classify the items, apply a policy per class, enter vendor terms and define the location network.

STEP 2 · WEEKLY

Set the demand plan

Maintain the budget and what-if scenarios, in Business Central or through Excel. Promote one to active.

STEP 3 · NIGHTLY

Run replenishment

Net requirements, consume the plan, explode bills of material, set safety stock, transfer first, build vendor orders, and raise exceptions.

STEP 4 · DAILY

Work the queues

Buyers and planners check the reasons, edit in place and approve. Approval creates standard orders.

STEP 5 · MONTHLY

Tune

Review fill rate, stock health and plan accuracy, and adjust classes and policies.

§ 06 · Built the Business Central way

No second system to look after

  • 01

    Native to Business Central

    Data, setup, screens and order creation all live in Business Central. Nothing syncs, and there is no second system of record.

  • 02

    Standard documents out

    It creates ordinary purchase, transfer and production orders. Your approvals and reports keep working.

  • 03

    Nothing overwritten quietly

    Imports and what-ifs make new versions. Promoting a plan is an explicit step, and it is recorded.

  • 04

    Works with your add-ons

    It depends on standard Business Central only. Add-ons, such as landed cost, connect through published events.

  • 05

    Your Azure, if you want it

    The optional statistical engine runs in your own tenant. Stag Head hosts nothing, and the product works fully without it.

  • 06

    Online or on-premises

    Runs in Business Central online and on-premises, from version 27 onwards, with English text that is ready for translation.
