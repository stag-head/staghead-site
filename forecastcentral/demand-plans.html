Part 03 · Planned

Budgets and scenarios

Many businesses plan from a budget, not from last year's sales. ForecastCentral treats the budget as a first-class input, keeps every version, and never changes the active plan without telling you.

RolesDemand planner, operations manager
EditionAll Business Central editions

§ 01 · What it does

Demand plans in practice

  • 01

    Versioned scenarios

    Budget, statistical and what-if plans, each with an owner and a status. Exactly one plan is active at a time.

  • 02

    Budget at any level

    Enter a budget by item category and month, then spread it down to items and locations by historical mix or a profile you choose.

  • 03

    Adjustments that explain themselves

    Uplifts, period shifts and flat weekly rates are stored as rules, so anyone can see why a number changed.

  • 04

    Compare and promote

    Put two scenarios side by side with the variance, then promote one. Promotion is an explicit, audited step.

  • 05

    Excel round trip

    Export to a fixed layout, change it in Excel, import it back. The import creates a new draft and shows you the difference before you accept it.

  • 06

    Bridge to Production Forecast

    Write the active plan to standard Production Forecast entries for companies moving across from the standard engine.

  • 07

    Budget accuracy over time

    The active plan is snapshotted every week, so accuracy and bias can be measured at one to five months ahead.

§ 02 · In a real situation

What if the big contract lands?

Example

The budget says 12,000 units for the year. Sales think a large new contract could add 15 percent from April.

The planner copies the budget into a what-if scenario, adds a 15 percent uplift from April as a rule, and compares the two. The engine can be run against the what-if without creating a single suggestion, so the purchasing impact is visible before anyone commits. If the contract is signed, the what-if is promoted and the change is on record.
